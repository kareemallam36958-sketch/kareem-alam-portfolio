    3Int. The script desk — late

    How a script
    leaves my desk.

    Not a wall of text. One document the client can approve and the crew can shoot from, on the same day.

    1. The one-page summary

      Brand, goal, audience, duration, platforms, format, style, dialect, key message and end line. All on page one, in one table.

    2. Scene by scene

      Each scene gets its goal, its timing, the shooting notes, the dialogue and the on-screen text, side by side.

    3. The cutdowns

      The 75-second master arrives with its 30 and 15-second versions already cut on paper.

    4. The shot list

      Every shot numbered, with its location and notes, so the shoot day holds no surprises.

    5. The clean dialogue

      A last page with dialogue only, ready for rehearsal and recording.

    P.S. There are plenty more scripts in the drawer. They stay there to protect the clients, the copyright and my lawyer's blood pressure. Ask nicely and I'll walk you through them.

    4Int. The process — continuous

    From coffee
    to final cut.

    1. 01

      Coffee

      Nothing gets written before the first cup. This is not a metaphor.

      1 cup, minimum
    2. 02

      The brief

      You talk, I listen, and ask “but why?” more often than a five-year-old.

      1 day
    3. 03

      The mix

      Two ideas that never met get locked in a room. One new idea walks out.

      2–4 days of staring at the ceiling
    4. 04

      The script

      Paper first, then Word, then a file called final_v7_REAL.docx.

      3–5 days
    5. 05

      Action!

      Camera, color, cut. Then we watch it together and pretend we're not proud.

      1–2 weeks
